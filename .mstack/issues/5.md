## Problem and goal

Describe recurring work and collaboration clearly without defining an orchestration engine.

## In scope

1. Refine routine purpose, prompt, intended trigger, participants and result when a real example needs clarification.
2. Describe responsibilities and handoff expectations in terms humans and agents can interpret using their own systems.
3. Keep routine prompts separate from standing instructions and reference shared skills.

## Exclusions

Schedulers, execution graphs, software-factory pipelines, runtime event protocols and activation.

## Acceptance criteria

1. A routine's prompt, trigger intent, participants and expected result can be understood without the source application.
2. A collaborative example makes each participant's responsibility and handoff expectations explicit.
3. Definitions do not require scheduler IDs, execution records or implementation-specific coordination machinery.

## Verification scenarios

1. **AC 1** — Read the routine and its linked resources and describe when work is intended, who does it and the expected result.
2. **AC 2** — Walk through a multi-agent example on paper; identify the next responsible agent and the information needed at each handoff.
3. **AC 3** — Inspect the example without a running system and confirm all required references resolve with no scheduler identity or run history.

## Constraints

Follow-up after the minimal routine and team example. Keep descriptive behavior distinct from enforced execution semantics. Preserve sources and source edits.

## Findings and open questions

Only add configuration where prose and existing references cannot express a concrete example clearly.
